Principles

01

Physical training

Classes, private lessons, and the park

Group classes cover technique, footwork and bouting. Private lessons are one-on-one with an épée or saber coach, at any level — to fix a weakness, sharpen your game, or prepare for a tournament. Twice a week we train outdoors in Minsheng Park: Wednesday mornings for footwork, Saturday mornings for cross-training. Everything runs in English and Chinese.

  • Group classes — technique, footwork, bouting
  • One-on-one lessons with an épée or saber coach
  • Park cross-training, Wednesday and Saturday mornings
  • Taught in English and Chinese
See the lessons
02

Mental training

Strategy, reflexes, and keeping your head

Bouts are decided by decisions. We drill strategy and tactical execution: reading the opponent, confirming the action before you commit, and the reflex cues that fire the right response without thinking. Every fencer trains toward a target outcome for the phrase, not just “get a hit”. Sessions include reaction-speed work. After a competition we watch your bouts back and go through what actually happened. The room stays positive: a bad result is not held against anyone.

  • Strategy and tactical execution — reading the opponent, choosing the action
  • Reflex cues and action confirmation, drilled until they fire without thinking
  • A target outcome for every phrase, and reaction-speed work every session
  • Video review of your own competition bouts
  • Mood control under pressure, in a room where a bad result is not held against you
See the tournament calendar
03

Data-driven

Online-first, and everything logged

The club runs online first: schedules, footage and your own record are all there, wherever you happen to be training. Every bout is scored and logged, and each fencer can see their own numbers. The training plan changes when the numbers say it should. The software we use and the wireless scoring hardware we built are open source, with the specs published, so another club can build the same thing without starting over.

  • Online-first — schedules, footage and your record travel with you
  • Every bout scored and logged; you see your own numbers
  • Open-source software and open hardware specs
  • Results published, wins and losses alike

    Questions

    Before you come in

    Do I need any experience?
    None. Most people start from zero, and you'll fence a real bout in your first session.
    Do I have to compete?
    After the beginner course, yes — it leaves you competition-ready. You don't have to win: pick an event, train for it and show up. We handle the registration details.
    What language are classes in?
    English and Mandarin. Announcements and course notes are in both.
    What should I wear and bring?
    Long sports pants (no shorts), indoor trainers, water and a towel. We provide all fencing kit. Tie back long hair; no jewelry on the hands.
    Is fencing safe? Is it safe for kids?
    It's safer than basketball or soccer. Blades are blunt and flexible, masks are rated to 1600 newtons, and nobody fences without full kit. Kids' lessons start at seven.
    How do I sign up?
    Message us on LINE, Instagram, WhatsApp or Facebook, or use the form below. We'll tell you where and when to come.
    Do you have parking? How do I get there?
    Three minutes' walk from MRT Nanjing Sanmin, exit 2. Scooter parking outside, a paid car park next door. Buses 262, 279 and 617 stop at the corner.
    I train at another club. Can I fence with you?
    Please do — open training, Friday club night and the park sessions are all open to you. Bring your kit (we'll lend what's missing) and message us first. We just ask that our fencers get the same welcome at your club.
    What is wireless scoring?
    Electric fencing normally wires each fencer to a scoring box through a reel. We use that, and we also built our own wireless units, so we can run scored bouts on any open floor. It keeps costs down.
    Where are the park sessions?
    Minsheng Park, near MRT Nanjing Sanmin: Wednesday mornings for footwork, Saturday mornings for cross-training. Open to anyone. Wear running shoes; rain plans go up on LINE by 6 a.m.
