--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY THIS DOCUMENT EXISTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The public site states three principles in about forty words each. This is the
long form behind them: what each pillar actually commits us to, broken into the
components that carry the commitment, and — for every component — the evidence
that lets us defend the claim to a prospective member, a parent, a partner
venue, a sponsor, a federation officer or a journalist.

A claim we cannot evidence is a claim we should stop making. Every component
below therefore carries an evidence list with a status tag. Where the tag is
not [HAVE], the claim is either softened on the public site or the evidence is
queued for collection; the gap list at the end of this document tracks that work.

EVIDENCE KEY
  [HAVE]        Exists now, held by the club, producible on request.
  [PARTIAL]     Exists but thin, undated, unreleased, or covering only part of
                the period we claim.
  [PLACEHOLDER] The public site currently shows sample or dummy material in
                this slot. The claim is NOT yet evidenced. Replace before use.
  [NEED]        Does not exist. To be created or collected.

Every photograph, video or quotation listed below may only be published once
the person in it has signed the media release (docs/media_release.txt). Minors
require a guardian signature. No exceptions, including for footage shot in a
public park.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MISSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Sheshouzuo Fencing Club makes competitive épée and saber fencing available to
anyone in Taipei who wants it: organized online, trained wherever we can secure
floor space, and priced so that the cost of a salle never decides who gets to
fence.

We opened in December 2019 under Sagittarius, the archer, and took the sign's
name: 射手座.

The three pillars are Physical training, Mental training and Data-driven, in the
order they appear on the public site. They are not ranked, and they are not
independent: the physical work is what makes the tactical work executable, the
mental work is what makes the physical work count under pressure, and the record
is what stops either of the first two becoming a story we tell about ourselves.
When the three conflict, the conflict is resolved in writing and the decision is
recorded here at the next revision.

A note on what changed in version 1.2. The previous three pillars were Open,
Competitive and Data-driven. Open and Competitive are no longer headings: their
components were not retired, they moved. The open-results and open-source
components sit inside Data-driven, which is broader than the old pillar of the
same name; the competition-preparation components sit under Standing
Commitments, because the public site still makes every one of those claims in
its lessons and tournament sections without calling them pillars. Physical
training and Mental training are genuinely new claims, and almost everything
under them is a gap. That is stated rather than disguised: a register whose new
pillars arrived pre-evidenced would not be worth keeping.

PILLAR 01 — PHYSICAL TRAINING
"Classes, private lessons, and the park"
================================================================================
Fencing is a sport before it is a game of chess, and the club's physical
provision is three things stacked: group classes for technique, footwork and
bouting; one-on-one lessons with a weapon specialist; and outdoor conditioning
in a public park twice a week. All of it runs in English and Chinese.

This pillar is the one a prospective member tests first and the one that is
easiest to evidence, because it is made of scheduled sessions that either happen
or do not. That also makes an unevidenced claim here unusually cheap to catch.

--- Component 1.1 — Group classes cover technique, footwork and bouting --------

Adult and children's group lessons are the spine of the week: guard and
distance, footwork, blade work, and supervised bouting rather than drill alone.
A member who attends only group classes should still be improving, which is the
test of whether the group session is a real training unit or a holding pattern
for people who cannot afford private lessons.

Evidence:
  [HAVE]     Course structure as published: adult beginner épée group lessons,
             children's beginner épée and saber (ages 7–13).
  [HAVE]     Weekly session pattern published in the club calendar
             (js/events.js)
             and surfaced on the site.
  [PLACEHOLDER] Every event currently in js/events.js is a sample. The real
                schedule must replace them before the calendar evidences that
                any of these sessions actually run.
  [NEED]     Written syllabus with the session-by-session progression. This is
             the document a parent asks for, and it is the same artifact 4.5
             needs.
  [NEED]     Attendance record for a representative term, which is what turns
             "weekly" from a claim into a fact.

--- Component 1.2 — One-on-one lessons with an épée or saber coach -------------

Private lessons are available at any level and are where an individual fault
gets fixed — the habit a fencer cannot see from inside the bout. Two weapons,
each led by a specialist: Eric Odle on épée, Yiling Ku on saber. The claim that
a lesson is worth its price rests entirely on the coach, so coaching
qualifications are evidence for this component rather than biography.

  [NEED]     Fencing coaching qualifications and certifications, with issuing

             a Taiwanese parent recognizes; neither coach holds it yet.
  [NEED]     Competitive records, honestly scoped.
  [NEED]     Coach portraits, with releases.
  [NEED]     警察刑事紀錄證明書 for both coaches, which Part B.7 of
             docs/minor_safeguarding_consent.txt promises guardians the club

--- Component 1.3 — Park cross-training, twice a week, open to anyone ----------

Wednesday mornings are footwork; Saturday mornings are cross-training. Both run
outdoors at Minsheng Park, Songshan, and both are open to people who are not
members — which is deliberate, because the park session is the cheapest way for
a curious person to meet the club without committing to anything.

  [NEED]     A written session plan for each of the two mornings, showing they

--- Component 1.4 — Everything runs in English and Chinese ---------------------

Classes, private lessons, announcements and course notes are delivered

PILLAR 02 — MENTAL TRAINING
"Strategy, reflexes, and keeping your head"
================================================================================
Bouts are decided by decisions taken under time pressure. This pillar covers the
tactical half of that — reading an opponent, confirming an action before
committing to it, the reflex cues that fire a trained response without
deliberation, and a target outcome for the phrase rather than a vague intent to
score — and the psychological half: staying level when the score turns, and a
room where a bad result is not held against anyone.

This is the pillar with the widest gap between what the site claims and what the
club can currently show. Most clubs leave all of it to chance and say nothing
about it; claiming it explicitly is a stronger position only if the claim can be
evidenced, and at version 1.2 almost none of it can be. Component 2.4 is the
exception, because it was already a practice under the previous Data-driven
pillar and moves across with its evidence intact.

--- Component 2.1 — Strategy and tactical execution are drilled, not assumed ---

Reading the opponent and choosing the action are trained explicitly, as their
own content, rather than left to emerge from bouting volume. The distinction
that matters is between a fencer who executes a technique well and one who knows
which technique this opponent has just made available.

Evidence:
  [HAVE]     The commitment is published on the site, which is itself evidence
             of the standard we have set ourselves.
  [NEED]     Session plans showing tactical content as a scheduled item with its
             own objective, distinct from technical drilling.
  [NEED]     A worked example: one opponent read, the action it opened, and the
             drill that trains it. One page would do.
  [NEED]     Some measure that the drilling transfers — the simplest honest one
             is whether the action chosen in the lesson is the action used in
             the bout, which requires the bout log at 3.2.

--- Component 2.2 — Reflex cues and action confirmation ------------------------

Two specific skills. A reflex cue is the trained trigger that fires the right
response without deliberation. Action confirmation is the discipline of
verifying what the opponent is actually doing before committing — the thing that
separates a decisive attack from a hopeful one. Both are trainable and both are
measurable, which is why they are named on the site rather than folded into
general coaching.

Evidence:
  [NEED]     The drills themselves, written down: what cue, what response, what
             the coach is watching for.
  [NEED]     Baseline and follow-up measurements for at least one cohort. A
             claim about reflexes that is never measured is a claim about
             coaching philosophy.
  [NEED]     A stated limit — the club makes no clinical or neurological claims
             about reaction training and refers members to qualified clinicians.
             This belongs in writing before the claim is promoted further.

--- Component 2.3 — Target outcomes, and reaction-speed work -------------------

Every fencer trains toward a defined outcome for the phrase rather than "get a
hit", and sessions include reaction-speed work as a scheduled component. The
target-outcome habit is what makes a bout reviewable afterwards: an intention
that was stated in advance can be checked against what happened, and one that
was not cannot.

Evidence:
  [HAVE]     Both commitments are published on the site.
  [NEED]     Evidence that reaction-speed work appears in sessions at the
             cadence the site implies — "every session" is a specific,
             falsifiable claim and is currently unsupported.
  [NEED]     A sample of stated target outcomes and their review, which is the
             artifact that joins this component to 2.4 and to the bout log.

--- Component 2.4 — Video analysis is a weekly meeting, not a private review ---
Footage is reviewed as a group, on a scheduled call, and the meeting is open to
every member rather than reserved for the squad. Two things follow from doing it
together rather than one-to-one. A fencer hears their own bout discussed in the
same register as everybody else's, which is what stops analysis feeling like a
verdict. And a coach's reading of a bout is stated in front of people who can
disagree with it, which is the only reliable check on it.

Evidence:
  [PARTIAL]  Bout footage is shot at open training and used for social posts; it
             has not been organized into an archive that analysis can draw on.
  [NEED]     Attendance record for the analysis call: date, who joined, what was
             reviewed. This is what turns the word "weekly" into a fact.
  [NEED]     One recorded session, with consent from everybody in it, usable as
             a sample of how the meeting runs.
  [NEED]     Signed media release for every fencer identifiable in footage shown
             in a group session, with a guardian signature for anyone under 18.
             Showing a bout to the membership is publication — see 1.1.
  [NEED]     The standing call details published to members, so that "open to
             every member" is verifiable rather than asserted.
  [NOTE]     Moved from Pillar 03 in version 1.2. The site now presents video
             review as mental training — reviewing the decision, not the
             statistic — which is where the practice always belonged.


--- Component 2.5 — Mood control under pressure --------------------------------

Staying level when the score goes against you is trained, and the same skill is
offered as transferable to any high-pressure day. The cultural half of this is
the club's own conduct: a poor result is not held against a member, and nobody
is written off after one. Coaches model it by naming what the coaching plan got
wrong before naming what the fencer got wrong.

Evidence:
  [HAVE]     Debrief practice — coaches name what the coaching plan got wrong
             first — is the club's current habit, and is the same practice
             evidenced under 3.4.
  [PLACEHOLDER] The member testimonials that would demonstrate this culture from
                the member's side are all sample copy, including the quotation
                describing a first ranking tournament at forty-one.
  [NEED]     One written post-tournament debrief, redacted, usable as a sample
             of how the club handles a poor result.
  [NEED]     Whatever mood-control work is actually coached, written down. At
             present the claim rests entirely on the club's culture being what
             it says it is, with nothing a sceptical reader could check.

PILLAR 03 — DATA-DRIVEN
"Online-first, and everything logged"
================================================================================
Fencing generates data whether or not anybody writes it down. Every bout ends in
a score, every touch has a cause, and every block of training either moves a
number or does not. Most clubs discard all of it and coach from memory and feel.
We record it, and we let what we find change the training plan. The commitment
is not that the club is scientific — it is that a claim about a fencer, a
drill or a season should be checkable against something other than a coach's
impression.

This pillar is broader in version 1.2 than the pillar of the same name in 1.1.
It absorbed the whole of the old Open pillar, because publishing results and
publishing tools are the same commitment seen from two sides: a record that only
the club can see is not a check on the club. That is also where this pillar
stops being comfortable. Publishing a result is easy when the result is a medal;
publishing the number that says a drill we have run for two years does nothing
is not.

Two constraints come with this pillar and are treated as part of it rather than
as small print. First, a member's competitive record is personal data: Taiwan's
Personal Data Protection Act applies to it, and footage shown in a group meeting
is a publication to an audience, so the media release (docs/media_release.txt)
governs it exactly as it governs anything we post. Second, a number that only
the coach sees is not evidence to the fencer it describes. Members see their own
record.

--- Component 3.1 — Online-first: the record travels with the member -----------

The club has floors all over Taipei and no single salle, so there is no
noticeboard everybody walks past. Schedules, venue changes, entry deadlines and
rain calls go out online first, to every member at the same moment, and a
member's own record is reachable wherever they happen to be training. Leaving
Taipei does not end a membership.

Evidence:
  [HAVE]     Club calendar infrastructure: one schedule covering weekly
             sessions, pop-up practices, tournaments and socials, with type
             filters and a details view (js/events.js, surfaced on the site).
  [HAVE]     LINE as the published primary channel, with Discord and WhatsApp
             for members and overseas members respectively.
  [PLACEHOLDER] Every event in js/events.js is a sample, so the calendar
                currently evidences the mechanism and not the practice.
  [NEED]     Whatever a member actually logs into. At version 1.2 there is no
             member-facing record system; "your record travels with you" is
             the intention behind 3.2 and cannot be evidenced before it.
  [NOTE]     The dedicated online-first section was removed from the public
             site on 18 September 2026. The claim survives as this pillar's
             headline, so it still needs evidencing.

--- Component 3.2 — Every bout scored and logged; the fencer owns the record ---
Bouts at open training and club night are scored and written down: date, weapon,
opponent, score, and the actions that produced the touches. The log is the spine
of the pillar — without it, video analysis has nothing to count against and the
sports-science reading has nothing to test. Each member can see their own record
in full, on request and without explaining why, and nobody's record is discussed
with other members except in the analysis sessions they are also in.

Evidence:
  [PARTIAL]  Our wireless scoring units produce a result for every bout run on
             them. Nothing currently captures that result beyond the box, so the
             raw material exists and the record does not.
  [NEED]     The bout log itself, in any durable form — a spreadsheet is enough
             to begin. Until it exists and has a start date, this component is
             an intention rather than a practice.
  [NEED]     One redacted member record covering a full season, usable as a
             sample of what a member is shown about themselves.
  [NEED]     A written data statement: what is recorded, where it is held, how
             long it is kept, who can see it, and how a member gets a copy or
             asks for deletion. A consent line belongs on the membership form.
  [HAVE]     The commitment is published on the site, which is itself evidence
             of the standard we have set ourselves.

--- Component 3.3 — Open-source software, open hardware specifications ---------
The club's software is released under an open-source license and the hardware
specifications for our scoring equipment are published. The target is a fully
DIY wireless reel and scoring box system built from off-the-shelf parts, with
the bill of materials, firmware and enclosure files public, so that a school
club or a group of students anywhere can put a scored piste on any flat floor
for a fraction of the commercial price. Publishing the design is how openness
and affordability turn out to be the same commitment seen from two directions:
the point of publishing is that cost stops being a barrier for someone else too.

Evidence:
  [PARTIAL] The wireless scoring units exist and are in use — they are cited in
            the site FAQ and in the Inclusive pillar as the reason we can run
            scored bouts on any open floor.
  [NEED]    Public repository for the firmware and the scoring software, with a
            license file. Until this is public the word "open-source" is a
            statement of intent, not a fact, and should be phrased that way.
  [NEED]    Published bill of materials and build notes for one reel and one
            scoring box, with a per-unit cost figure.
  [NEED]    Photographs of the units in use at a session, and one photograph of
            the internals for the build documentation.
  [NEED]    Reliability record: sessions run on DIY units, failures, and what
            was changed. Publishing the failures is the point.

--- Component 3.4 — Results are published, wins and losses alike ---------------
Training is recorded and posted rather than kept inside the room. Bouts from
open training, drills from group lessons and results from every tournament our
fencers enter go out on the club's public channels on a predictable rhythm, so
that a stranger can judge the club by what it does on a normal Tuesday rather
than by what its marketing says. This is also how an online-first club with no
single home salle stays legible to people who have not yet walked into a
session: the feed is the front door.

Evidence:

  [PLACEHOLDER] YouTube embed on the site still carries the literal string

  [NEED]        Posting-cadence record: a simple log of what went out, on which

  [NEED]        Archive of bout video from open training, with releases on file
                for every fencer identifiable in frame.
  [HAVE]        The commitment is stated publicly on the site, which is itself
                evidence of the standard we have set ourselves.
  [NOTE]     Merged with the former Component 1.2 (losses reviewed as openly as
             wins) in version 1.2; its evidence list follows below and is part

--- Component 3.5 — The plan changes on the evidence, including against us -----
The test of the pillar is not that we collect numbers. It is that a drill, a
block or a season plan has been changed because the record contradicted what we
believed — and that this is written down when it happens. This is Component 1.2
turned from a posture into a measurement: reviewing a loss openly is worth
something only if the review is against a record, and if the plan can lose the
argument.

Evidence:
  [NEED]     At least two documented cases of a training block or drill changed
             because the record said so, with the figure before and after.
  [NEED]     One documented case where the evidence contradicted a coach and the
             coach's plan changed. Without one, the pillar is decoration.
  [HAVE]     Debrief practice — coaches name what the coaching plan got wrong
             before they name what the fencer got wrong — is the club's current
             habit and is recorded under 1.2.

--- Component 3.6 — What we learn is shared with the community -----------------
Coaching notes, refereeing explanations, rule changes and tournament logistics
are written up for everyone, not circulated privately among members. The club
treats the Taiwanese fencing community as the audience rather than the
competition. In practice this is the tournament desk's by-product: once we have
read an organizer's announcement closely enough to brief our own fencers, the
briefing costs nothing to publish.

Evidence:

            deadline reminders, registration and kit-check help, carpools.
  [NEED]    Public archive of those briefings — the version other clubs' fencers
            can read.
  [NEED]    Record of the reciprocity pledge being honored in both directions:
            visits received from other clubs, visits our fencers made elsewhere,
            with dates.
  [PLACEHOLDER] The testimonial from "Kuo H.", the guest from a Hsinchu club, is
                sample copy. A real guest quotation is the strongest possible
                evidence for this component.

================================================================================
STANDING COMMITMENTS — ON THE SITE, NOT A PILLAR
================================================================================
Two former pillars, Inclusive and Competitive, are no longer headings. The
public site still makes every claim below — low cost in the hero copy, the
loaner kit and the welcome to visiting fencers in the FAQ, the beginner course
and private lessons in the lessons section, the tournament desk in its own
section — and the rule of this document is that a claim on the site is evidenced
here or is not made. They are kept in full, with their evidence and their gaps.
Retiring a heading did not retire the commitments; if the club means to drop any
of them, that is a separate decision and the public copy has to change with it.

--- Component 4.1 — Cost is engineered down, not discounted --------------------
We do not run a cheap version of an expensive club. The cost structure is
different at the root: footwork and cross-training happen in public parks,
bouting happens at a partner venue and at pop-up sessions around Taipei, and
scoring runs on units we built ourselves alongside conventional box-and-reel.
Removing the permanent salle removes the single largest fixed cost in the
sport, and that saving is what keeps membership reachable.

Evidence:
  [HAVE]     Park sessions published: Minsheng Park, Songshan, Wednesday and
             Saturday mornings, open to anyone, rain plans posted on LINE by
             6 a.m.
  [HAVE]     Partner venue published: B1, No. 128, Section 4, Bade Road,
             Songshan District, Taipei 105.
  [PARTIAL]  DIY wireless scoring in service alongside box-and-reel.
  [NEED]     The actual comparison: our per-month cost to a member against the
             Taipei market rate. Without the number this component is a feeling.
  [NEED]     Photographs of a park session and of a pop-up session on an
             unconventional floor, with releases.
  [NEED]     Written record of the venue partnerships and their terms.

--- Component 4.2 — Nobody is turned away for lack of kit ----------------------
A beginner needs nothing to start. Full loaner kit is provided, and guests from
other clubs are lent whatever they are missing. Equipment is one of the two
questions that stops a curious person from ever attending a first session; the
other is not knowing whether they will be welcome, which Component 4.3 covers.

Evidence:
  [HAVE]     Loaner kit commitment published on the site and in the FAQ.
  [NEED]     Inventory: sets held, by size, by weapon, with condition and last
             safety inspection date. This doubles as risk-management evidence
             supporting the liability waiver (docs/liability_waiver.txt), and is
             tracked for renewal in docs/credentials.md.
  [NEED]     Kit maintenance and retirement record — when a mask or jacket was
             last tested and when it was taken out of service.
  [NEED]     Photograph of the loaner rack.

--- Component 4.3 — The door is open to every club, and to every background ----
Our door is open to your fencers; we hope yours is open to ours. Guests train at
open training, Friday club night and the park sessions on a LINE message, and
the only thing asked in return is reciprocity. The same posture applies inside
the club: members come from fourteen countries, the club runs bilingually in
English and Chinese, and adults starting from zero in their forties are a normal
part of a beginner cohort rather than an exception anyone remarks on.

Evidence:
  [HAVE]     Reciprocity pledge published on the site and in the FAQ.
  [HAVE]     Bilingual operation — the entire public site carries both English
             and Traditional Chinese for every string, and this mission
             statement exists in both languages.
  [NEED]     "Members from 14 countries" is no longer published anywhere on
             the site — the club statistics went with the club section on 18
             September 2026. Either the figure is republished with a date
             attached, or this component rests on the membership records
             alone, which should be able to substantiate it on request.
  [PLACEHOLDER] The "Kuo H." guest testimonial and the "Daniel C." late-starter
                testimonial are both sample copy.
  [NEED]     Guest log: visiting fencers received, their home clubs, dates; and
             the reciprocal visits our members made.
  [NEED]     Age and starting-level distribution of current members, to
             substantiate the late-starter claim honestly.

--- Component 4.4 — Large, open spaces, secured with partners ------------------
Inclusion has a floor-space requirement. Six pistes' worth of usable space is
what lets a session take everyone who turns up rather than rationing strip time
to the most committed, and securing that space through partnerships rather than
a lease is what keeps it affordable. Space is therefore treated as an inclusion
issue and tracked as one.

Evidence:
  [NEED]     Six pistes is no longer published anywhere on the site. It needs
             tying to a specific venue and date to mean anything, whether or
             not it is republished.
  [NEED]     Written partnership terms for each venue, including capacity.
  [NEED]     Attendance-against-capacity record: how often a session ran at a
             level where somebody had to wait.
  [NEED]     Wide photograph of a full session showing the space in use.

--- Component 4.5 — The beginner course ends competition-ready -----------------
Adult and children's beginner group lessons are designed so that a graduate can
walk into a domestic tournament and function: guard, distance, parries, attacks,
and — the part most beginner courses omit — enough refereeing knowledge to
follow a director's calls and to sit the strip when asked. "Competition-ready"
is a testable claim, not a slogan: the test is that the graduate enters an event
and is not lost there.

Evidence:
  [HAVE]     Course structure as published: adult beginner épée group lessons,
             children's beginner épée and saber (ages 7–13), private lessons at
             any level.
  [NEED]     Written syllabus with the session-by-session progression and the
             graduation criteria. This is the document a parent asks for.
  [NEED]     Conversion figures: of the beginners who completed a course, how
             many entered a tournament within six months. Even a small honest
             number beats an unevidenced claim.
  [PLACEHOLDER] "Daniel C." (first ranking tournament eight months in) and
                "Sarah R." (training plan and a first Taiwanese tournament entry
                within a month of arriving) are both sample testimonials.
  [NEED]     Photographs of a beginner cohort at graduation and of a graduate at
             their first event, with releases signed.

--- Component 4.6 — The tournament desk tracks the season for the member -------
Tracking the calendar is a membership service, not the member's homework. We
watch federation and organizer announcements, read the rules, explain
eligibility, categories and fees, post deadline reminders on LINE two weeks and
three days out, help first-timers through registration and kit check, and
organize carpools and shared rooms on Discord. The failure mode we are
engineering against is specific and common: a fencer who was ready, wanted to
go, and missed the entry deadline.

Evidence:
  [HAVE]     Scope of the service, published on the site.
  [HAVE]     Club calendar infrastructure — events carry a registration object
             and surface automatically in the tournament desk list
             (js/events.js).
  [PLACEHOLDER] Every event currently in js/events.js is a sample. The real
                season must replace them before the calendar evidences anything.
  [NEED]     Deadline-reminder log: event, deadline, dates the two reminders
             went out, number of members entered.
  [NEED]     Missed-deadline count. If it is zero, that is the headline number
             for this entire pillar. If it is not, we publish it anyway — see
             Component 1.2.

--- Component 4.7 — Training is planned around the member's next event ---------
A member's training block is written against a specific date on the calendar.
What we drill in the eight weeks before a ranking event differs from what we
drill in an off-period, and the member knows which block they are in and why.
Private lessons exist primarily to serve this: fix the weakness that the last
competition exposed, in time for the next one.

Evidence:
  [NEED]     Two or three anonymized example training blocks, each tied to a
             named event, showing the progression.
  [NEED]     Coach's record of which members have a current target event.
  [PLACEHOLDER] "Sarah R." testimonial (training plan within a month of
                joining).
  [PARTIAL]  Private lesson offering is published; its role in competition
             preparation is stated on the site.

--- Component 4.8 — Sports science is read and discussed as a group ------------
Current work on training load, recovery, reaction time, movement and skill
acquisition is circulated and then discussed, with the discussion open to
members and not only to coaches. The point of reading it as a group is that most
of what is published is either not about fencing or not about people training
four hours a week, and deciding which applies to us is a judgement that should
be made out loud. A coach who says "the research says" without the group having
read it has not raised the standard of the argument, only its volume.

Evidence:
  [NEED]     Reading list with dates: what was circulated, when, and what it
             changed — or that it changed nothing, which is also a result.
  [NEED]     A written summary of at least one session where the group concluded
             a study did not transfer to us, and said why. That is the evidence
             that the discussion is real and not a conclusion handed down.
  [PARTIAL]  Coaching qualifications are the credential behind reading this
             literature critically; they are tracked in docs/credentials.md and
             are themselves still a gap on the public site (see 2.4).
  [NEED]     A stated limit: the club is not a research body, makes no medical
             or nutritional claims, and refers members to qualified clinicians.
             This belongs in writing before the claim is promoted further.
